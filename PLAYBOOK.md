# CHESS.md — A Harness thought experiment

> Build a chess game in Go that I can play in a browser at `https://chess.karstenjakobsen.dk:8001`. Open TCP port 8001 with UFW on my VPS. Use my DigitalOcean account for the `karstenjakobsen.dk` zone to point the hostname at the VPS, and use a Let’s Encrypt certificate. Track every game I play. My long-term goal is to win at least 50% of the time.

This scenario gives us a way to evaluate future Harness slices. Can the operator take an owner’s request, choose skills, delegate work, inspect the result, and decide what to do next? Can the owner later start from a goal and see who did what, under which grant, and with what evidence?

Assume the owner selects the VPS and wants to track games played in this app. Games played elsewhere would require another source of results.

## What goals would the operator propose?

The chess project has one delivery goal, two standing goals, and a delivery subgoal supporting the win-rate goal. The proposed measurement plan for each revision identifies the evidence to collect and the conditions for operator review.

| Objective | Lifetime | Clarification or agreed scope | Evidence collection (when, how, by whom) | When the operator acts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **I can play chess in a browser.** | One-time delivery | Who do you want to play against? The owner has specified the browser URL. | At handoff the worker submits the source revision, image digest, and browser test report. The test plays a legal move, rejects a move that leaves the king in check, and finishes a game against the chosen opponent. | Review the handoff once against the agreed opponent and play criteria. |
| **My chess game stays available over HTTPS on port 8001.** | Standing | Agree on the probe interval, how late a result can be, and when to investigate. Two consecutive failures is an example threshold. | On schedule, an external probe resolves the hostname, validates the certificate on port 8001, and loads the playable UI. The collector records the timestamped probe result. | Open one review when the failure threshold is reached or a probe is overdue. Repeated failures update that review. A passing probe clears the alert silently. |
| **I win at least 50% of my games.** | Standing | Which player, opponents, games, and window count? Does the owner want coaching? One possible target is 10 wins in the last 20 completed games against the computer at normal difficulty, with draws included. | When a qualifying game finishes, the collector records its source game ID and result. Harness updates the wins, losses, draws, and percentage. Until the agreed window fills, it reports the partial record. | By default, update progress silently. If the owner requests coaching, open one review when a full window first falls below target. The goal stays active when met. |
| **Record every finished game exactly once.** | One-time subgoal of the win-rate goal | Every game finished in this app must survive an offline operator session and repeated reads. Which games qualify for the win rate is a separate decision. | At handoff the worker submits a test report listing games finished while the operator was offline, the IDs returned by two reads, and the resulting distinct count. | Review the handoff once. Missing or double-counted games require follow-up. |

**Scale acceptance for a future slice.** Given 10,000 green standing goals, automated checks update progress and start zero operator runs. If one endpoint crosses its failure threshold, Harness opens one review even if the next ten probes also fail.

## What gets built, and where does it run?

A **project** holds the chess application’s source and build definition. A **skill** gives the operator or worker versioned instructions. A **connector** implements operations against a service. A **connection** binds a connector to an owner-configured destination and credential. A **host capability** performs a specific operation on a named machine, such as ensuring a firewall rule.

A worker can build a missing connector or host helper and demonstrate it against a disposable target. The owner grants the operator authority to use the reviewed capability against the real destination. The chess build worker can deliver its artifact without access to the DigitalOcean account or the VPS firewall.

**MCP baseline at Slice 9.** Operators already have `list_skills`, `read_skill`, `get_goal_progress`, and, under a parent-scoped grant, `create_subgoal`. Slice 9 provides a Python execution backend for batch workloads. The other MCP actions below, worker access to skills, and long-lived service deployment are proposals.

| Scenario and objective | Clarification or agreed scope | What gets built and where it runs | Who has authority and which actions they use | Evidence collection (when, how, by whom) | When the operator acts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Chess project** — deliver a playable build. | Reuse the delivery goal’s agreed opponent and play criteria. | The worker builds the Go app, browser UI, tests, `Dockerfile`, and game-history schema. Git holds the source and the registry holds the image. | Operator uses `create_subgoal`, `list_skills`, `read_skill`, and `start_work` with the revision, brief, and pinned skills. Worker uses `get_work_brief`, `read_skill`, and `submit_work_result` in the assigned workspace. | At handoff the worker submits the browser test report, commit, and digest. Reading a skill requires no separate observation. | Start the build and review the handoff once. Reuse that report for the delivery goal. |
| **Deployment** — run the pinned build and retain games across replacements. | The owner selects the VPS and Nomad target. Updates reuse the service ID and persistent volume. | The worker supplies the service definition. Nomad supervises the Go container on the VPS under its own service identity. | Operator has a service-scoped grant for `deploy_service`, `inspect_service`, and `stop_service`. | After deployment or update, the deployment connector records the allocation and running digest reported by Nomad and compares the digest with the handoff. | Initiate an authorized deployment or update. Investigate a failed rollout or digest mismatch. A matching digest requires no further review. |
| **DNS and TLS** — serve the chess UI at the requested HTTPS URL. | The owner selects the DNS connection. The grant covers the chess address record and its ACME challenge TXT record, including renewal. | DigitalOcean hosts the records. The VPS ACME client creates and removes the DNS-01 challenge record. The TLS proxy serves port 8001 and forwards to the private chess listener. [DNS-01 challenge](https://letsencrypt.org/docs/challenge-types/#dns-01-challenge) | Operator uses the Harness connector’s `get_record` and `ensure_record` via `invoke_connector`, and `ensure_certificate` for issuance and renewal. Credentials and private keys stay with the connection and ACME client. | After setup or renewal, an external `check_https` probe verifies DNS, the served certificate, and the UI. The collector also records scheduled probes for the availability goal. | Configure once. A successful renewal is silent. An endpoint failure follows the availability goal’s review threshold. |
| **Host firewall** — allow inbound TCP 8001. | The named VPS and port are fixed. Automated setup requires an existing admin connection authorized by the owner. | A worker can test the helper on a disposable host. A provisioning job uses the admin connection to install the reviewed, root-owned helper and a sudoers rule for a dedicated account on the VPS. | The provisioning job holds the admin credential. The operator’s host grant permits `inspect_firewall_rule` and `ensure_firewall_rule` for TCP 8001. | At setup or after a change, the helper reads back the active UFW rule. Harness records the helper as observer and the operator as requester for operator-initiated checks. | Request the rule for deployment. Follow up if setup fails or the rule is missing. Successful readback ends the task. |
| **Game monitoring** — collect each result once and keep the win rate current. | The feed covers the owner’s player. The goal revision defines qualifying opponents, window, draws, and whether to request coaching. | The chess service persists game ID, player, opponent, result, and finish time. A collector resumes the feed between operator sessions. | The collector uses a player-scoped read-only connection for `list_finished_games(player_id, after_cursor)`. The operator can inspect progress with `get_goal_progress`. | The collector calls `record_observation` for unseen games under its own identity and attributes results to the chess service. Harness counts distinct source IDs. A repeated read adds no evidence. | A new game updates progress silently. Request coaching review only on the agreed below-target condition. An idle player requires no action. |
